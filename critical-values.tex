\begin{center}
  {\Large \textsc Every Algebraic Number is a
    Critical Value of an Integer Polynomial}

  {by RJ Acu\~na}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
  \begin{minipage}{0.85\textwidth}
    \begin{abstract}
      \noindent
      \justifying
      For every nonconstant $f \in \Z[X]$ we construct $g \in \Z[X]$ and an
      irreducible $H \in \Z[X]$ with $H \mid g'$ and $H^{2} \mid f \circ g$.
      Equivalently, every algebraic number is a critical value of an integer
      polynomial, so the set of critical values of $\Z[X]$ is exactly
      $\overline{\Q}$. The construction is explicit, and the proof is
      root-free: no algebraic closure, no minimal polynomials and no splitting
      fields appear in it, only two exact identities in $\Z[X]$ and an
      induction on the prime factors of an integer. Algebraic numbers enter
      only in the statement of the corollary. The argument has been formalized
      in Lean~4 and the construction implemented as a program.
    \end{abstract}
  \end{minipage}
\end{center}

\section{Introduction}

A critical value of $g \in \Z[X]$ is a number $\alpha$ with $g(\beta) = \alpha$
and $g'(\beta) = 0$ for some $\beta$. Since $g' \neq 0$ in characteristic zero,
its roots are algebraic and $g$ carries algebraic numbers to algebraic numbers,
so every critical value of every nonconstant $g \in \Z[X]$ is algebraic. This
note proves the converse, and does so with an explicit construction.

\begin{theorem}\label{T1}
  Let $f \in \Z[X]$ be nonconstant. Then there exist $g, H \in \Z[X]$ with $H$
  irreducible in $\Z[X]$ and $1 \leq \deg H$, such that
  \[
    H \mid g' \quad\text{and}\quad H^{2} \mid f \circ g \qquad \text{in }\Z[X].
  \]
  The $g$ produced satisfies $\deg g \geq 2$.
\end{theorem}

Two corollaries follow at once. The first is the divisibility statement in the
form one usually wants it.

\begin{cor}\label{C2}
  With $g$ as in Theorem \ref{T1}, write $g' = c \prod_{i} g_{i}$ with each
  $g_{i}$ irreducible in $\Z[X]$ and $c \in \Z$. Then $g_{i}^{2} \mid f \circ g$
  for at least one $i$.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
  $H$ is irreducible, hence prime in the unique factorization domain $\Z[X]$,
  and $\deg H \geq 1$, so $H \nmid c$ (as a constant polynomial). From $H \mid c \prod_{i} g_{i}$ we get
  $H \mid g_{i}$ for some $i$; two irreducibles with one dividing the other are
  associates, and associates have associate squares, so
  $g_{i}^{2} \mid H^{2} \mid f \circ g$.
\end{proof}

The second is the statement in the title.

\begin{cor}\label{C3}
  For every $\alpha \in \overline{\Q}$ there are $g \in \Z[X]$ and
  $\beta \in \overline{\Q}$ with $g(\beta) = \alpha$ and $g'(\beta) = 0$.
  Consequently the set of critical values of nonconstant integer polynomials is
  exactly $\overline{\Q}$.
\end{cor}

The point of the proof below is what it does \emph{not} use. One expects a
question about algebraic numbers to be answered by working in a number field:
choose $\alpha$, take its minimal polynomial, look for a critical point among
the conjugates. That route runs into the unit group and the class group of
$\Q(\alpha)$ almost immediately, and into the question of whether
$\mathcal{O}_{K}$ is monogenic. None of that is needed. The construction is a
single pair of polynomial identities, valid in $\Z[X]$ with no reference to any
root of anything, followed by an elementary descent that removes an unwanted
integer factor. Roots appear exactly once, in \S\ref{Scrit}, to convert the
divisibility statement into the statement about critical values.

There is one structural warning, which \S\ref{Sscope} makes precise: $H$ cannot
be required to be monic. The monic form of Theorem \ref{T1} is false, and the
obstruction is not a matter of a harder search.

\subsection{Conventions}

Throughout, $p' = \operatorname{derivative} p$, and for a fixed nonzero
$M \in \Z$ we write
\[
  \dil{p} := p(M X) = p \circ (M X),
\]
the \emph{dilation} of $p$. Dilation is a ring homomorphism $\Z[X] \to \Z[X]$,
it is injective for $M \neq 0$, it preserves degree, and it interacts with the
derivative by the chain rule
\begin{equation}\label{Echain}
  (\dil{p})' = M \, \dil{p'} .
\end{equation}
These four facts are used on essentially every line of \S\ref{Sident} and are
worth isolating before anything else; the factor $M$ in \eqref{Echain} is the
reason $M$ has to appear in the statement of Lemma \ref{LA}.

\section{The construction}\label{Scons}

Fix a nonconstant $f \in \Z[X]$ and suppose $X \nmid f$; the excluded case is
settled separately in \S\ref{Sdegen}. All of the following data is explicit and
computable.

\begin{center}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{llX}
    \textit{Symbol} & \textit{Definition} & \textit{Notes}\\[2pt]
    \hline
    $h$        & a primitive irreducible factor of $f$ with $h(0) \neq 0$ & in $\Z[X]$\\
    $f_{1}$    & the cofactor, $f = h f_{1}$ & in $\Z[X]$\\
    $n$        & $\deg h \geq 1$ & \\
    $h_{0}$    & $h(0) \neq 0$ & in $\Z$\\
    $u, v, \rho$ & any B\'ezout data, $u h + v h' = \rho$, $\rho \neq 0$ & $u,v \in \Z[X]$, $\rho \in \Z$\\
    $M$        & $\rho\, h_{0}$, so $M \neq 0$ & the dilation factor\\
    $H$        & $h(MX)/h_{0}$ & $H(0) = 1$, $\deg H = n$\\
    $W$        & $\bigl(v(0) H - v(MX)\bigr)/\rho$ & $W(0) = 0$\\
    $g$        & $M X + h(MX)\, W \;=\; M X + h_{0} H W$ & \\
    \hline
  \end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Existence of $h$.} $f$ is nonconstant, so it has an irreducible
factor. Irreducible factors of positive degree in $\Z[X]$ are primitive, and
$h(0) \neq 0$ because $h(0) = 0$ would give $X \mid h \mid f$, excluded.

\paragraph{Existence of the B\'ezout data.} $h$ is irreducible and primitive in
$\Z[X]$, hence irreducible in $\Q[X]$ by Gauss's lemma. In characteristic zero
$h' \neq 0$ and $\deg h' < \deg h$, so $h \nmid h'$ and therefore
$\gcd(h, h') = 1$ in the principal ideal domain $\Q[X]$. B\'ezout there and
clearing denominators produces $u, v \in \Z[X]$ and $\rho \in \Z \setminus
\{0\}$.

Any nonzero $\rho$ in the elimination ideal $(h, h') \cap \Z$ works. The
resultant $\operatorname{Res}(h,h')$ is one admissible choice and is never
needed as such; smaller choices give a smaller $g$, and the generator of the
elimination ideal --- the \emph{reduced} resultant --- is the best available.
Example \ref{Ex82} below uses $\rho = 2$ where the resultant is $4$.

\subsection{Integrality}\label{Sint}

The two divisions in the table are exact, which is the whole reason $M$ was
defined as the product $\rho h_{0}$ rather than as either factor alone.

\begin{lemma}[Integrality]\label{LI}
  $H \in \Z[X]$ with $H(0) = 1$ and $\deg H = n$; and $W \in \Z[X]$ with
  $W(0) = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Write $h = \sum_{j} h_{j} X^{j}$ and $v = \sum_{j} v_{j} X^{j}$. The
  coefficients of $H$ are $H_{0} = h_{0}/h_{0} = 1$ and, for $j \geq 1$,
  \[
    H_{j} \;=\; \frac{h_{j} M^{j}}{h_{0}}
          \;=\; \frac{h_{j}\, \rho^{j} h_{0}^{j}}{h_{0}}
          \;=\; h_{j}\, \rho^{j} h_{0}^{\,j-1} \;\in\; \Z ,
  \]
  using $h_{0} \mid M$. Since $\deg h = n$ and $M \neq 0$, $\deg H = n$.

  For $W$, the coefficient of $X^{j}$ in $\rho W = v(0) H - \dil{v}$ is
  $v(0) H_{j} - v_{j} M^{j}$. At $j = 0$ this is $v_{0} \cdot 1 - v_{0} = 0$,
  which gives both integrality there and $W(0) = 0$. For $j \geq 1$ the display
  above exhibits $\rho^{j} \mid H_{j}$, and $\rho \mid M^{j}$, so $\rho$ divides
  the whole coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
  For the proof of Theorem \ref{T1} it is cleaner to treat $H$ and $W$ as
  \emph{data} subject to the defining identities
  \begin{equation}\label{Edef}
    h_{0} H = \dil{h}, \qquad \rho W = v(0) H - \dil{v},
    \qquad H(0) = 1, \qquad W(0) = 0,
  \end{equation}
  rather than as quotients. Nothing after this point uses anything else about
  them, and no division in $\Z[X]$ ever has to be justified again. Lemma
  \ref{LI} is then exactly the statement that such data exists.
\end{rem}

\section{The two identities}\label{Sident}

Both statements below are exact identities in $\Z[X]$. There are no
congruences, no roots and no primality anywhere in this section; in particular
\emph{irreducibility of $h$ is never used here}. It is needed only to produce
the B\'ezout data in \S\ref{Scons} and again in \S\ref{Sirr}.

Dilating the B\'ezout relation $u h + v h' = \rho$ by the ring homomorphism
$p \mapsto \dil{p}$ gives the form in which it will be used,
\begin{equation}\label{Ebez}
  \dil{u}\,\dil{h} + \dil{v}\,\dil{h'} = \rho,
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  \dil{v}\,\dil{h'} = \rho - h_{0} \dil{u} H ,
\end{equation}
the second form by $\dil h = h_{0}H$.

\begin{lemma}[Derivative]\label{LA}
  $\rho\, g' = H A$, where
  $A := M h_{0} \dil{u} + M v(0) \dil{h'} + \rho h_{0} W' \in \Z[X]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  From $g = MX + \dil{h} W$ and the chain rule \eqref{Echain},
  \[
    g' \;=\; M + M \dil{h'} W + \dil{h}\, W' .
  \]
  Multiply by $\rho$ and substitute $\rho W = v(0)H - \dil{v}$ and
  $\dil h = h_{0} H$:
  \[
    \rho g'
      \;=\; \rho M + M \dil{h'}\bigl(v(0) H - \dil{v}\bigr) + h_{0} H \rho W'
      \;=\; \rho M - M \dil{h'}\,\dil{v} + H\bigl(M v(0) \dil{h'} + \rho h_{0} W'\bigr).
  \]
  By \eqref{Ebez}, $\rho M - M \dil{h'} \dil{v} = \rho M - M(\rho - h_{0}\dil{u}H)
  = M h_{0} \dil{u} H$, and collecting the factor $H$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Value]\label{LB}
  $\rho\,(f \circ g) = H^{2} D$ for an explicit $D \in \Z[X]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Put $B := h_{0} H W$, so that $g = MX + B$. By Taylor to second order
  (Lemma \ref{LT}) there is $k \in \Z[X]$ with
  \[
    f \circ g \;=\; \dil{f} + \dil{f'} B + B^{2} k .
  \]
  Since $f = h f_{1}$ we have $\dil f = \dil h\, \dil{f_{1}} = h_{0} H
  \dil{f_{1}}$, and $B^{2} = h_{0}^{2} H^{2} W^{2}$, so
  \begin{equation}\label{Estar}
    f \circ g \;=\; h_{0} H \bigl(\dil{f_{1}} + \dil{f'} W\bigr)
                 \;+\; H^{2}\bigl(h_{0}^{2} W^{2} k\bigr).
  \end{equation}
  It remains to extract one more factor of $H$ from the first bracket. Dilating
  the product rule $f' = h' f_{1} + h f_{1}'$ and multiplying by $\dil v$,
  \[
    \dil{f'}\,\dil{v}
      \;=\; \dil{h'}\,\dil{v}\,\dil{f_{1}} + \dil{h}\,\dil{f_{1}'}\,\dil{v}
      \;=\; \bigl(\rho - h_{0}\dil{u}H\bigr)\dil{f_{1}} + h_{0} H \dil{f_{1}'}\,\dil{v},
  \]
  using \eqref{Ebez} again. Hence
  $\rho \dil{f_{1}} - \dil{f'}\dil{v}
    = h_{0} H\bigl(\dil{u}\,\dil{f_{1}} - \dil{v}\,\dil{f_{1}'}\bigr)$, and so
  \[
    \rho\bigl(\dil{f_{1}} + \dil{f'} W\bigr)
      \;=\; \rho \dil{f_{1}} + \dil{f'}\bigl(v(0)H - \dil{v}\bigr)
      \;=\; H C, \qquad
    C := h_{0}\bigl(\dil{u}\,\dil{f_{1}} - \dil{v}\,\dil{f_{1}'}\bigr) + v(0) \dil{f'} .
  \]
  Multiplying \eqref{Estar} by $\rho$ and substituting gives
  $\rho (f \circ g) = H^{2} D$ with $D := h_{0} C + \rho h_{0}^{2} W^{2} k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Taylor to second order]\label{LT}
  Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $f \in \Z[X]$ and $a, b \in R$. Then there is
  $k \in R$ with $f(a + b) = f(a) + f'(a)\, b + b^{2} k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Both sides are additive in $f$ and the statement is stable under
  multiplication by a constant, so it suffices to check $f = X^{m}$, where the
  binomial theorem gives
  $(a+b)^{m} = a^{m} + m a^{m-1} b + b^{2}\sum_{i \geq 2}\binom{m}{i}a^{m-i}b^{i-2}$.
  Applied with $R = \Z[X]$, $a = MX$ and $b = B$, this is the form used in
  Lemma \ref{LB}.
\end{proof}

\section{Descent: removing \texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{rho}}\label{Sdesc}

Lemmas \ref{LA} and \ref{LB} give the desired divisibilities only after
multiplication by $\rho$. Removing it is where the normalization $H(0) = 1$
earns its place.

\begin{lemma}[Descent]\label{LC}
  Let $P, K \in \Z[X]$ and $\rho \in \Z$, $\rho \neq 0$, and suppose $K(0)$ is a
  unit of $\Z$. If $K \mid \rho P$ in $\Z[X]$ then $K \mid P$ in $\Z[X]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  By strong induction on the number of prime factors of $|\rho|$. If
  $\rho = \pm 1$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose a prime
  $p \mid \rho$ and write $\rho = p \rho_{1}$, and let $\rho P = K D$. Reducing
  modulo $p$ gives $0 = \overline{K}\,\overline{D}$ in $\F_{p}[X]$. Now
  $K(0) = \pm 1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$, so $\overline{K} \neq 0$; and
  $\F_{p}[X]$ is an integral domain, so $\overline{D} = 0$, that is
  $D = p D_{1}$. Cancelling $p$ in the domain $\Z[X]$ leaves
  $\rho_{1} P = K D_{1}$, and $|\rho_{1}|$ has one prime factor fewer.
\end{proof}

\begin{cor}\label{CC}
  $H \mid g'$ and $H^{2} \mid f \circ g$, both in $\Z[X]$.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
  Apply Lemma \ref{LC} to Lemma \ref{LA} with $K = H$, whose constant
  coefficient is $1$; and to Lemma \ref{LB} with $K = H^{2}$, whose constant
  coefficient is likewise $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
  This is why the construction normalizes $H(0) = 1$ instead of making $H$
  monic. Two other routes reach Corollary \ref{CC}. One: $H(0) = 1$ makes $H$ a
  unit in the power series ring $\Z[[X]]$, so
  $(f \circ g) H^{-2}$ lies in $\Z[[X]]$; it also equals
  $D/\rho \in \Q[X]$; and $\Q[X] \cap \Z[[X]] = \Z[X]$. Two:
  $H$ is primitive and irreducible, hence prime in $\Z[X]$, and $H \nmid \rho$
  by degree. The second route needs \S\ref{Sirr} first, while the induction
  above and the power series argument need nothing.
\end{rem}

\section{Nondegeneracy and irreducibility}

\subsection{Nondegeneracy}\label{Snondeg}

\begin{lemma}\label{LE}
  $W \neq 0$, $\deg W \geq 1$, $\deg g = n + \deg W \geq 2$, and $g' \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Suppose $W = 0$. Then $\dil{v} = v(0) H$, and comparing with
  $h_{0} H = \dil{h}$ gives $h_{0} \dil{v} = v(0)\, \dil{h}$; since dilation is
  injective, $h_{0} v = v(0) h$. Then $h \mid h_{0} v$ in $\Q[X]$, so
  $v = (v(0)/h_{0}) h$, and the B\'ezout relation collapses to
  $\rho = h\bigl(u + (v(0)/h_{0}) h'\bigr)$, forcing $\deg h = 0$ and
  contradicting $n \geq 1$. So $W \neq 0$, and $W(0) = 0$ then gives
  $\deg W \geq 1$.

  Since $M \neq 0$ we have $\deg \dil{h} = n$, so
  $\deg(\dil{h} W) = n + \deg W \geq n + 1 \geq 2 > 1 = \deg(MX)$. There is
  therefore no cancellation in $g = MX + \dil h W$ and $\deg g = n + \deg W$.
  Finally $\deg g \geq 2 \geq 1$ and characteristic zero give $g' \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

If the B\'ezout data is chosen with $\deg v < n$, which is automatic for the
subresultant construction, then $\deg W \leq n$ and hence
\[
  n + 1 \;\leq\; \deg g \;\leq\; 2n .
\]
The lower bound is not an artifact: $H \mid g'$ with $\deg H = n$ forces
$\deg g' \geq n$ for \emph{any} solution whatsoever, so no construction can do
better. Example \ref{Ex82} attains it.

\subsection{Irreducibility}\label{Sirr}

\begin{lemma}\label{LD}
  $H$ is irreducible in $\Z[X]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  $h$ is primitive and irreducible in $\Z[X]$, hence irreducible in $\Q[X]$ by
  Gauss's lemma. For $M \neq 0$ the dilation $X \mapsto MX$ is a
  $\Q$-algebra automorphism of $\Q[X]$, so $\dil{h}$ is irreducible in $\Q[X]$;
  and $H = \dil{h}/h_{0}$ differs from it by a unit of $\Q$, so $H$ is
  irreducible in $\Q[X]$. By Lemma \ref{LI}, $H(0) = 1$, so $H$ is primitive.
  Primitive and irreducible over $\Q$ gives irreducible in $\Z[X]$, by Gauss's
  lemma again.
\end{proof}

This is the only place Gauss's lemma is used, and $\Z$ enters the argument only
here and in the prime factorization of Lemma \ref{LC}. Everything else goes
through over any characteristic zero unique factorization domain.

\section{The degenerate case}\label{Sdegen}

Suppose $X \mid f$. Then
\[
  g = X^{2}, \qquad H = X
\]
settles Theorem \ref{T1}: $g' = 2X$ is divisible by $H$; writing $f = X f_{2}$
gives $f \circ g = X^{2}\,(f_{2} \circ X^{2})$, which is divisible by
$H^{2} = X^{2}$; and $X$ is irreducible in $\Z[X]$ of degree $1$, with
$\deg g = 2$.

Note that the case split is on $X \mid f$, not on $f$ being a monomial: this
branch covers $f = X(X^{2}+1)$ as well as $f = cX^{k}$. Equivalently, the split
is on whether $f$ has \emph{some} irreducible factor with nonzero constant term.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{T1}]
  If $X \mid f$, use \S\ref{Sdegen}. Otherwise take $h$, and B\'ezout data, as
  in \S\ref{Scons}, and define $M, H, W, g$ by the table; these lie in $\Z[X]$
  by Lemma \ref{LI}. Then $H$ is irreducible by Lemma \ref{LD} with
  $\deg H = n \geq 1$, the two divisibilities hold by Corollary \ref{CC}, and
  $\deg g \geq 2$ by Lemma \ref{LE}.
\end{proof}

\section{Critical values}\label{Scrit}

Only now do roots appear.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary \ref{C3}]
  Let $\alpha \in \overline{\Q}$. If $\alpha = 0$, take $g = X^{2}$ and
  $\beta = 0$. Otherwise let $h$ be the primitive integral minimal polynomial of
  $\alpha$, so $h(0) \neq 0$, and run \S\ref{Scons} with $f = h$, hence
  $f_{1} = 1$. Set
  \[
    \beta := \alpha / M .
  \]
  Then $H(\beta) = h(M\beta)/h_{0} = h(\alpha)/h_{0} = 0$. Evaluating the
  B\'ezout relation at $\alpha$ and using $h(\alpha) = 0$ gives
  $v(\alpha) h'(\alpha) = \rho$, so
  \[
    W(\beta) = \frac{v(0) H(\beta) - v(\alpha)}{\rho}
             = -\frac{v(\alpha)}{\rho}
             = -\frac{1}{h'(\alpha)} ,
  \]
  which is defined because $h$ is separable. Therefore
  \[
    g(\beta) = M\beta + h(\alpha) W(\beta) = \alpha, \qquad
    g'(\beta) = M + M h'(\alpha) W(\beta) + h(\alpha) W'(\beta)
              = M(1 - 1) = 0 .
  \]
  So $\beta$ is a critical point of $g$ with critical value $\alpha$. The
  converse was observed at the start of \S1.
\end{proof}

Two features of this are worth naming. First, $\beta$ is the \emph{same}
rescaling $\alpha/M$ for every root $\alpha$ of $h$ simultaneously: a single $g$
has all $n$ conjugates as critical values, at the $n$ points $\alpha_{i}/M$.
Second, the ring at work is $\Z[\beta] = \Z[\alpha/M] \supseteq
\Z[\alpha][1/\rho]$. Dividing $\alpha$ by $M$ is what makes the higher Taylor
coefficients integral, while $H(0) = 1$ makes $\beta$ a \emph{unit} in
$\Z[\beta]$, and that is what permits trading the constant term of
$1/h'(\alpha)$ for higher degree terms.

What does \emph{not} appear is as much to the point: no class group of
$\Q(\alpha)$, no unit group $\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}$, no monogenicity of
$\mathcal{O}_{K}$, and no Thue equation or index form. $\Z[\beta]$ is not an
order in $\mathcal{O}_{K}$ --- $\beta$ is not an algebraic integer --- and the
units the argument consumes are $S$-units manufactured by the construction
itself.

For a reducible $f$ the statement reduces to the irreducible case for a reason
worth stating explicitly, since it is easy to mis-handle: a root $\alpha$ of $f$
is a root of \emph{some} irreducible factor $h$ of $f$, and the construction run
on that $h$ produces a $g$ having $\alpha$ as a critical value. Different
factors give different $g$. It is not the case that one $g$ serves all of a
reducible $f$.

\section{Sharpness and scope}\label{Sscope}

\paragraph{$H$ cannot be required monic.} The monic form of Theorem \ref{T1} is
false. Suppose $H$ were monic irreducible with $g(\beta) = \sqrt{2}$ and
$g'(\beta) = 0$; then $H^{2} \mid g^{2} - 2$ in $\Z[X]$. Modulo $2$,
$\overline{H}^{2} \mid \overline{g}^{2}$ gives $\overline{H} \mid \overline{g}$,
so $g = HA + 2B$ for some $A, B \in \Z[X]$. Then
\[
  g^{2} - 2 = H^{2}A^{2} + 4HAB + 4B^{2} - 2,
\]
so $H^{2} \mid 4HAB + 4B^{2} - 2 = 2C$ with $C := 2HAB + 2B^{2} - 1$ and
$\overline{C} = 1$. As $H$ is monic, division gives $2C = H^{2}D$; reducing mod
$2$ forces $\overline{D} = 0$, so $D = 2E$ and $C = H^{2}E$, whence
$1 = \overline{H}^{2}\overline{E}$ --- impossible for $\deg \overline{H} \geq 1$.
So $\sqrt 2$ is not a critical value at any algebraic integer critical point.

Yet $-4\sqrt{2}$ is, and monically: with $g = X^{3} - 6X$ and $H = X^{2} - 2$,
\[
  g' = 3(X^{2}-2) = 3H, \qquad
  g^{2} - 32 = (X^{2}-2)^{2}(X^{2}-8) = H^{2}(X^{2}-8),
\]
so $H^{2} \mid f \circ g$ for $f = X^{2} - 32$. The invariant separating the two
cases is the class of $d\alpha$ in $\Omega_{\Z[\beta]/\Z} \cong
\Z[\beta]/(H'(\beta))$; it is neither integrality nor a unit condition.

\paragraph{Higher multiplicity is free.} Since $H'(\beta)$ is a unit in
$\Z[\beta]$, the map $\Z[X] \to \Z[\beta][Y]/(Y^{m})$ sending $X \mapsto
\beta + Y$ is surjective, so $H^{m} \mid f \circ g$ is achievable for every
$m \geq 1$. Theorem \ref{T1} is the case $m = 2$, which is the one the
critical value statement needs.

\paragraph{Hypotheses that are not needed.} Nothing requires $f$ to be
separable, squarefree or primitive, and $\deg f$ is unbounded; a repeated factor
of $f$ only helps. Characteristic zero, on the other hand, is essential, and
enters through $h' \neq 0$. In characteristic $p$ with $\alpha$ inseparable the
approach dies at $\rho = 0$, and the statement itself degenerates: $g = X^{p}$
gives $f \circ g = f^{p}$, but $g' = 0$ has no irreducible factorization.

\section{Examples}\label{Sex}

Each line below is a closed identity, checkable by expansion.

\begin{example}[$f = qX - p$, $p \neq 0$]
  With $u = 0$, $v = 1$, $\rho = q$, $h_{0} = -p$, $M = -pq$, $H = q^{2}X + 1$
  and $W = qX$,
  \[
    g = -pq^{3}X^{2} - 2pqX, \qquad
    g' = -2pq\,(q^{2}X+1), \qquad
    f \circ g = -p\,(q^{2}X+1)^{2}.
  \]
\end{example}

\begin{example}[$f = X^{2}+1$]\label{Ex82}
  With $u = 2$, $v = -X$, $\rho = 2$, $h_{0} = 1$, $M = 2$, $H = 4X^{2}+1$ and
  $W = X$,
  \[
    g = 4X^{3} + 3X, \qquad
    g' = 3(4X^{2}+1), \qquad
    f \circ g = (4X^{2}+1)^{2}(X^{2}+1).
  \]
  Here $\deg g = 3 = n + 1$ attains the lower bound of \S\ref{Snondeg}. Note
  also $\rho = 2$ while $\operatorname{Res}(h,h') = 4$: the reduced resultant is
  the better choice. The unit group $\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times} = \{\pm 1, \pm i\}$
  has rank $0$, and it is $\Z[i/2] = \Z[i][1/2]$ that supplies the needed unit
  $1+i$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[$f = X^{3}-2$]
  With $u = 3$, $v = -X$, $\rho = -6$, $h_{0} = -2$, $M = 12$,
  $H = 1 - 864X^{3}$ and $W = -2X$,
  \[
    g = -3456X^{4} + 16X, \qquad g' = 16\,(1 - 864X^{3}).
  \]
\end{example}

\begin{example}[$f = X^{3}-X-1$]
  The construction gives $M = 23$ and a sextic. A hand-tuned quintic also
  witnesses the \emph{statement}, and is a useful independent check of it:
  \[
    g = -24334X^{5} + 39675X^{4} - 26450X^{3} + 9085X^{2} - 1610X + 118,
    \qquad H = 23X^{3} - 23X^{2} + 8X - 1,
  \]
  with $g' = -230\,(23X-7)H$ and $H^{2} \mid f \circ g$, the cofactor having
  degree $9$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Dedekind's cubic]\label{Ex85}
  $f = X^{3} - X^{2} - 2X - 8$ is the standard example of a field whose ring of
  integers is not monogenic: $2$ is a common index divisor \cite{dedekind1878}.
  The construction runs unchanged, which is the point of including it --- no
  step of \S\ref{Scons}--\S\ref{Sdesc} is sensitive to monogenicity.

  It is also where formalization corrected the author. An earlier draft of this
  material asserted $M = 4024$ here. That is unattainable. Since $M = \rho h_{0}$
  and $h_{0} = -8$, it would need $\rho = -503$, the discriminant of the field;
  but every B\'ezout constant for this $h$ is even. Writing
  $h = X^{2}(X-1) - 2(X+4)$ and $h' = 3X^{2} - 2(X+1)$ puts any relation
  $uh + vh' = c$ into the shape
  \[
    X^{2}\bigl(u(X-1) + 3v\bigr) \;=\; c + 2\bigl(u(X+4) + v(X+1)\bigr),
  \]
  and reading off the constant coefficient gives $0 = c + 2\bigl(u(X+4) +
  v(X+1)\bigr)(0)$, so $2 \mid c$. In fact $(h,h') \cap \Z = (1006)$, with
  certificate
  \[
    (125 - 21X)\,h + (3 - 44X + 7X^{2})\,h' = -1006 ,
  \]
  so the smallest attainable $|M|$ is $8048$. The relevant invariant is the
  reduced resultant, not $\operatorname{disc}(K) = -503$; here
  $\operatorname{disc}(h) = -2012$ and the index is $2$.
\end{example}

\section{Formalization}\label{Sformal}

The development above has been formalized in Lean~4 \cite{lean4} against
Mathlib \cite{mathlib}, in about $1{,}200$ lines. The main statement is

\begin{lstlisting}[language=,caption={}]
theorem main (f : Z[X]) (hf : 1 <= f.natDegree) :
    exists g H : Z[X], Irreducible H /\ H | derivative g /\ H^2 | f.comp g
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent
with a strengthened form carrying $2 \leq \deg g$ and $1 \leq \deg H$, and with
Corollaries \ref{C2} and \ref{C3} proved from it. Every result depends only on
the three standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}.

The organization follows the sections above: the dilation and its chain rule
\eqref{Echain} first, then \texttt{Setup}, a structure carrying the data of
\S\ref{Scons} with \eqref{Edef} as hypotheses, then Lemmas \ref{LA} and \ref{LB}
as \texttt{linear\_combination} calls from those hypotheses, then descent,
irreducibility and nondegeneracy. Treating $H$ and $W$ as data rather than as
quotients, as in Remark 1, is what keeps \S\ref{Sident} free of any reasoning
about divisibility in $\Z[X]$; the integrality of Lemma \ref{LI} is discharged
once, in the existence proof for \texttt{Setup}.

Three things were genuinely easier on paper than in Lean, and are recorded in
case they are useful to someone attempting a similar development. Dilation as a
ring homomorphism, and its interaction with \texttt{derivative}, is used
everywhere and deserves to be isolated first. Lemma \ref{LT} is stated in
Mathlib for \texttt{eval} rather than for \texttt{comp}, and has to be
transported. And Mathlib has the translation $X \mapsto X + c$ as an algebra
equivalence but not the dilation $X \mapsto cX$, which had to be built to prove
Lemma \ref{LD}.

The one place where the formalization changed the mathematics is Example
\ref{Ex85}, whose erratum is itself machine-checked.

\section{Computation}\label{Scomp}

The construction is effective, and a program implementing it accompanies this
note. It takes $f \in \Z[X]$, factors it, and for each irreducible factor $h$
returns $M$, $H$, $W$, $g$ and a verification of every hypothesis and conclusion
of \S\ref{Scons}--\S\ref{Sirr} on the actual output.

Two remarks on what the computation costs. The B\'ezout data is obtained by the
extended Euclidean algorithm in $\Q[X]$ followed by content reduction, which
yields the generator of $(h,h') \cap \Z$ --- the reduced resultant, and so the
smallest $|M|$ available. Factoring $f$ is the expensive step and is delegated
to FLINT \cite{flint}, which implements van Hoeij's knapsack recombination
\cite{vanhoeij2002} in the improved form of \cite{hnvh2011}. Root finding, used
only for display, is the Aberth--Ehrlich iteration \cite{aberth1973}, the method
underlying MPSolve \cite{mpsolve}.

The coefficients grow quickly, and it is worth being blunt about the size. Since
$\deg g = n + \deg W \leq 2n$ and the coefficients of $H$ carry $\rho^{j}
h_{0}^{j-1}$, the output is large even for small input: $f = x^{5}-x-1$ gives a
$g$ of degree $10$ with $34$-digit coefficients, and $f = x^{10} + 20x^{4} +
x^{3} - 10x^{2} + 1$ gives degree $20$ with $376$-digit coefficients. This is
not a defect of the implementation; it is the size of the answer.
